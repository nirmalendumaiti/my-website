TEACHING VISION

Cultivating Critical & Spatial Consciousness

My classroom operates as an active forum for inquiry where literary texts are not passive objects of analysis, but dynamic spaces reflecting environmental, historical, and postcolonial realities.

01

Ecocritical & Ecological Literacy

Encouraging students to read with an ecological lens—examining how representations of land, water, more-than-human world, and climate crisis shape narratives and cultural imagination.

02

Postcolonial & Spatial Perspective

Guiding learners to unpack colonial geographies, displacement, and borderlands, fostering a nuanced understanding of power dynamics in global and regional literatures.

03

Contextual Close Reading

Balancing rigorous textual and stylistic analysis with historical, cultural, and environmental contextualization, moving beyond surface-level plot summaries.

04

Dialogic & Student-Centered Dialogue

Prioritising interactive discussions, seminar presentations, and collaborative workshops that empower students to formulate independent critical arguments.

CURRICULUM & SYLLABI

Courses Taught

POSTGRADUATE (MA) ENG-PG-IKS

Indian Knowledge System

Introducing major Indian philosophical, literary, and aesthetic traditions to examine how knowledge is produced, preserved, interpreted, and transmitted through texts, oral narratives, and cultural practices.

Exploring Indian literary theories, particularly rasa, dhvani, and alaṅkāra, to understand the relationship between language, literary expression, and aesthetic experience.

Examining representations of nature, landscapes, and human–nonhuman relationships in Indian knowledge traditions, with particular attention to ecological ethics, cultural memory, and indigenous understandings of place.

Key Writers & Themes

Indian Aesthetics & Literary Theory Philosophy, Ethics & Ways of Knowing Nature, Place & Cultural Knowledge

Modules: Introduction to Green Studies, Anthropocene Narratives, Island & Coastal Vulnerabilities, Eco-violence & Displacements.

POSTGRADUATE (MA) ENG DSC 601

Homi K. Bhabha: “The Other Question”

Examining Bhabha’s critique of colonial discourse to understand how stereotypes construct and sustain colonial authority through the representation of the colonised as culturally inferior, fixed, and different.

Exploring the concepts of stereotype, ambivalence, and mimicry to analyse the contradictions within colonial power and the instability of colonial identities.

Analysing how representations of cultural difference shape relations of power, identity, and belonging, with particular attention to the ways colonial discourse produces and reinforces the binary between the coloniser and the colonised.

Key Writers & Themes

Postcolonial Theory and Colonial Discourse Stereotype, Ambivalence, and Mimicry Cultural Difference and Identity

Modules: Orientalism & Cartography, Decolonising Mind & Space, Subaltern Voices in Tribal Narratives, Diaspora & Belonging.

UNDERGRADUATE (BA HONS) ENGHMJ-01

History of English Literature & English Language: Restoration Period & Eighteenth Century Literature

Introducing major authors and examining their contributions to the development of English literature.

Exploring the relationship between the popularity of urbane literature and the rise of the middle class.

Examining how literary representations of nature and material progress reflect changing attitudes towards the natural world during the Industrial Revolution, including the treatment of nature as a resource for mass production and the longer-term ecological and climatic consequences of industrialisation.

Key Writers & Themes

John Dryden Alexander Pope Daniel Defoe Dr. Samuel Johnson

Modules: Pre-Independence Novel, Modern Indian Poetry, Regional Consciousness in Translation, Gender and Family Dynamics.

UNDERGRADUATE (BA HONS) ENGHMJ-03

Jonathan Swift: Gulliver's Travels (Book 1 & 2)

Examining Swift’s satire of human exceptionalism, political power, and European claims to civilisation through Gulliver’s encounters with Lilliput and Brobdingnag.

Exploring the relationships between humans, animals, bodies, and environments to question anthropocentric assumptions and the boundaries between the human and the nonhuman.

Analysing how scale, spatial difference, and encounters with unfamiliar worlds unsettle established hierarchies of power, knowledge, and belonging.

Key Writers & Themes

Jonathan Swift and Satire Human and Nonhuman Worlds Space, Scale, and Otherness

Modules: Romantic Ecology, Urban Wastelands, Modernist Narrative Techniques, Post-War Literary Shifts.

UNDERGRADUATE (BA HONS) ENGMJE-03

James Joyce: "Araby"

Examining Joyce’s use of first-person narration, symbolism, and epiphany to represent the gap between youthful idealism and lived experience.

Exploring Dublin as an urban space shaped by religious conventions, social constraints, and the monotony of everyday life.

Analysing how desire, disillusionment, and the protagonist’s encounter with the bazaar reveal the influence of cultural and material environments on individual identity and belonging.

Key Themes

Stream of Consciousness Epiphany Desire and Disillusionment

Modules: Young Adult Literatures.

SPECIAL TUTORIAL (MA) ENG-PG-Special Tutorial

Postcolonial Ecocriticism

Introducing postcolonial ecocriticism as an interdisciplinary approach that examines the intersections of colonialism, environmental exploitation, and the unequal distribution of ecological resources and risks.

Exploring how colonial histories, capitalist development, and anthropocentric worldviews shape representations of landscapes, indigenous communities, and human–nonhuman relationships in literary texts.

Analysing how literature exposes environmental injustice, displacement, and competing claims to land and belonging while foregrounding indigenous knowledge, ecological resistance, and alternative ways of imagining human–nature relationships.

Key Focus Areas

Elizabeth DeLoughrey, Graham Huggan, Helen Tiffin, and Rob Nixon Colonialism, Landscape, and Displacement Indigenous Knowledge and More-than-Human Worlds

Modules: Postcolonial Ecocriticism

STUDENT MENTORSHIP

Skill Enhancement Courses

Beyond the regular lecture hours, I actively guide the undergraduate students in various papers on the Skill Enhancement Course (SEC).

SEC 1: Soft Skills: Guiding the first semester students of English Honours course on Personality Development, Emotional Intelligence, Leadership & Team Building, and Problem Solving.
SEC 2: Basic Phonetics: Guiding the second semester students of English Honours course on Air-Stream Mechanism, Vowel & Consonant Sounds, Syllable, Word Accent, Rhythm, and Intonation.
SEC 3: Creative Writing: Guiding the third semester students of English Honours course on the Art and Craft of Writing, Creative Writings on Poetry, Fiction, & Drama.

Pedagogical Philosophy Statement

“Teaching literature in today's world requires us to bridge the textual with the terrestrial. When students learn to recognise the environmental and postcolonial geographies embedded in stories, they transform from passive readers into conscious stewards of culture and ecology.”
