Background

Narratives of Belonging: Research & Teaching

The dialogue between narrative, culture, environment and place draws me to the world of literature. For me, literature is more than the stories it tells; it is an ongoing engagement with the worlds we inhabit, the landscapes we shape, and the relationships we forge with the more-than-human world.

My research brings together environmental humanities, postcolonialism, and spatial studies to address a question that continues to interest me: “Where do we belong?” The search for an answer often leads me to adopt interdisciplinary approaches beyond the boundaries of conventional research.

I always find myself at home when I sit at my writing desk or when I stand before a classroom of students. As a teacher, I try to enkindle in my students a spark of curiosity that literature naturally nourishes. I encourage them to question, think critically, and find their own paths into research. My classroom is a space for exploration—a laboratory in which to experiment with new and unconventional approaches to literary interpretation.

My research in literature has taken me to different corners of the world. I have met wonderful people, experienced unique cultures, and shared memorable conversations. These experiences have added new dimensions to the way I think about literature and space. Turning the pages of a new book, scribbling my thoughts on paper, and reflecting on lives and experiences that often go unnoticed are among the things that keep me close to literature. Beneath these everyday pleasures lies a deeper sense of hope—a hope for humanity and the more-than-human world, and for the possibility of understanding our place within it.

Research Interests

Questions That Guide My Work

My research is driven by questions about how literature responds to changing environments, contested spaces, and histories of colonial and postcolonial transformation.

I am particularly interested in the ways literary narratives make visible relationships between people, places, landscapes, and ecological systems.

Teaching

The Classroom as Dialogue

I approach teaching as an opportunity for dialogue and shared inquiry. Literature becomes a way of asking questions about culture, society, history, environment, and the world we inhabit.

I aim to create classrooms where students can develop critical reading practices while also connecting literary study with questions that matter beyond the classroom.

Research

Reading Across Boundaries

My research practice moves across disciplinary and geographical boundaries, bringing literary studies into conversation with ecology, environmental humanities, postcolonial studies, and spatial thinking.

This interdisciplinary approach allows literary texts to be read not only as cultural objects but also as records of changing relationships between communities, environments, histories, and places.
